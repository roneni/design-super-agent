# Curated Color Palettes

## Purpose
Ready-to-use color palettes organized by mood and genre. Each palette includes the emotional effect, recommended usage, and what makes it work. These are not random combinations — they are proven palettes derived from successful designs.

---

## Dark & Cosmic

### Midnight Nebula
- Background: #0a0a0a, Surface: #141420, Elevated: #1a1a2e
- Primary accent: #7c3aed (electric violet), Secondary: #22d3ee (cyan), Tertiary: #ec4899 (hot pink)
- Text: #f0f0f0 primary, #a0a0b0 secondary
- **Why it works:** Deep blue-black backgrounds create infinite depth. The violet-cyan-pink triad references UV-reactive visuals. High saturation accents on near-black feel like light sources rather than paint.
- **Use for:** Music portals, festival sites, gaming, nightlife, psytrance, EDM
- **Signature move:** Use accents only on interactive elements and focal points. Backgrounds stay dark and quiet.

### Deep Space
- Background: #050510, Surface: #0d0d1a, Elevated: #16162a
- Primary: #6366f1 (indigo), Secondary: #8b5cf6 (purple), Accent: #f59e0b (amber)
- Text: #e2e8f0 primary, #94a3b8 secondary
- **Why it works:** The warm amber accent against cold indigo creates visual tension — like a distant star in void. Your eye goes straight to the amber.
- **Use for:** Astronomy, sci-fi, tech products, data visualization, space-themed brands

### Obsidian Fire
- Background: #0a0a0a, Surface: #1a1210, Elevated: #2a1a14
- Primary: #ef4444 (red), Secondary: #f97316 (orange), Accent: #fbbf24 (gold)
- Text: #fef2f2 primary, #b0907a secondary
- **Why it works:** Warm spectrum on dark ground feels like embers — primal, grounding, earthy. The surface tint shifts from neutral black to warm brown-black, creating organic warmth.
- **Use for:** Outdoor festivals (Ozora/Boom vibe), campfire culture, artisan products, warm nightlife

## Light & Clean

### Paper White
- Background: #fafafa, Surface: #ffffff, Elevated: #f5f5f5
- Primary: #171717 (near-black), Secondary: #525252 (gray), Accent: #2563eb (blue)
- Border: #e5e5e5
- **Why it works:** Maximum readability. The blue accent is the only color, making every interactive element instantly obvious. Feels authoritative and professional.
- **Use for:** Documentation, SaaS dashboards, enterprise tools, editorial content

### Warm Cream

- Primary: #292524 (warm black), Secondary: #78716c (warm gray), Accent: #b45309 (amber)
- Border: #e7e5e4
- **Why it works:** The warm undertone throughout (stone colors instead of neutral grays) feels human and approachable. Less clinical than pure white.
- **Use for:** Lifestyle brands, food/beverage, wellness, luxury retail, editorial

### Arctic Mint

## Vibrant & Energetic

### Electric Pop
- Background: #ffffff, Surface: #fafafa
- Primary: #7c3aed (violet), Secondary: #06b6d4 (cyan), Accent: #f43f5e (rose)
- Gradient: linear-gradient(135deg, #7c3aed, #06b6d4)
- **Why it works:** High-saturation triadic palette with maximum visual energy. Works because the white background provides breathing room — the colors would clash on dark.
- **Use for:** Creative agencies, social media, youth-focused products, events, marketing

### Sunrise Gradient
- Primaries: #f97316 (orange) → #ec4899 (pink) → #8b5cf6 (purple)
- Background: #0f0f0f dark variant, #ffffff light variant
- Supporting: #fbbf24 (yellow), #14b8a6 (teal)
- **Why it works:** Follows the natural warm-to-cool transition of a sunrise sky. The gradient direction matters — warm colors at top/left (light source), cool at bottom/right.
- **Use for:** Wellness apps, meditation, creative platforms, music streaming, festival brands

### Neon Tokyo
- Background: #0a0a0a, Surface: #141414
- Primary: #00ff88 (neon green), Secondary: #ff0080 (neon pink), Accent: #00ccff (neon blue)
- Glow effect: 0 0 20px rgba(0, 255, 136, 0.3)
- **Why it works:** The glow effects on dark background literally simulate neon lights. Extremely high contrast. Use sparingly — this palette is a shout, not a whisper.
- **Use for:** Cyberpunk, gaming, nightlife, tech products, hacker culture

## How to Use These Palettes

1. **Never use more than one palette.** Pick one, commit.
2. **60-30-10 rule:** 60% background/surface, 30% secondary/text, 10% accent
3. **Accent is precious.** If everything is accent-colored, nothing stands out. Reserve for CTAs, active states, focal points.
4. **Dark palettes need more spacing.** Light text on dark backgrounds requires larger text, more line-height, and more breathing room.
5. **Test with real content.** A palette that looks great in a swatch grid can fail when applied to actual UI with text, images, and interactive elements.
