# Taste Calibration

## Purpose
Taste is the ability to distinguish quality tiers — not just "good vs bad" but "competent vs professional vs exceptional vs masterclass." This module teaches the agent to perceive and aim for the highest quality tier it can reach, and to recognize when its output falls short.

**Core principle**: Knowledge isn't a static ladder — it's recursive. Each level internalized becomes the new floor, revealing a new ceiling that was previously imperceptible. A Type I civilization can't conceptualize Type III. The stock-level designer literally CANNOT see what's wrong with their work — the flaws are above their perception threshold. This agent must push that threshold as high as possible.

## General Quality Tier Definitions

### Stock-Level (40-50)
- Template with brand colors swapped. Generic Pexels/Pixabay imagery
- Default spacing, default border-radius, default everything
- "Looks like a website" but nothing more. Indistinguishable from 10,000 similar sites
- **Visual indicators**: Bootstrap/Tailwind defaults visible. Hero section with stock photo of diverse people at a table. Blue CTA button. White background. Generic sans-serif font at default sizes
- **What's missing**: Any indication that a human made an intentional design decision. Every choice is a default

### Competent (55-65)
- Custom palette (not defaults), intentional font pairing, modified spacing
- Clear hierarchy, content is organized, nothing is broken
- Missing refinements: spacing is close but inconsistent (24px here, 20px there), colors are close but not from a system (manually picked, not derived from a scale)
- **Visual indicators**: The design has a "direction" but doesn't fully commit. Some sections feel polished, others feel unfinished. The overall impression is "someone tried"
- **What's missing**: A system. A reason WHY these choices were made. The feeling that every decision connects to a central intention

### Professional (70-80)
- Clear design system: consistent spacing scale, color palette with hierarchy, type scale with clear ratios
- Safe but solid choices. Nothing is wrong, but nothing surprises
- The designer knows the rules and follows them faithfully. Could be a mid-level designer at a good agency
- **Visual indicators**: 8px grid alignment, consistent border-radius, proper color contrast ratios. The design "works" at every breakpoint. Nothing feels accidental
- **What's missing**: The "unsafe" choice. The one element that takes a risk — an unusual layout, a surprising color accent, a typographic moment that breaks convention. The design is correct but not memorable

### Exceptional (85-95)
- Every decision is intentional and interconnected — change one element and the whole system shifts
- One "how did they do that?" moment per page — a gradient that feels three-dimensional, a scroll animation that reveals content in a way that creates wonder, a typographic treatment that becomes the brand
- The design has a POINT OF VIEW — you can sense the designer's perspective and personality
- **Visual indicators**: You notice things on the 3rd and 4th viewing that you missed before. Details reward close inspection. The spacing isn't just consistent — it BREATHES. The color isn't just harmonious — it creates MOOD
- **What's missing**: Almost nothing. At this tier, gaps are matters of personal taste, not objective failure. The difference between 85 and 95 is polish — subpixel alignment, shadow consistency, the "last 5%" that separates great from perfect

### Masterclass (95-100)
- The design IS the brand. Remove the logo and the design is still recognizable
- Every pixel is intentional. The amount of negative space, the specific opacity of a shadow, the exact easing curve of a transition — all tuned with obsessive care
- Creates imitators — other designers study and reference this work
- **Visual indicators**: The design sets trends rather than following them. It establishes new conventions that others adopt. It feels both inevitable ("of course it should look like this") and surprising ("I've never seen this before")

## Cosmic/Psytrance Quality Tiers

### Stock-Level Cosmic (40-50)
- Dark background (#111111) + generic starfield (white dots randomly placed) + purple gradient + geometric pattern overlaid at high opacity
- The "Canva space template" aesthetic. Recognizable immediately as generic
- **Specific failure markers**: Gradient is exactly purple→blue or purple→pink. Stars are uniform size. No atmospheric depth — everything feels the same distance away. Sacred geometry is a PNG overlay, not integrated

### Competent Cosmic (55-65)
- Intentional dark palette (e.g., #0a0a1a → #1a1a2e), custom star animation (varying sizes, twinkle), 1-2 glow effects
- But: composition feels flat. One visual layer only. The nebula is a background IMAGE, not a layered atmosphere. Sacred geometry exists but doesn't interact with other elements
- **Specific failure markers**: No foreground elements. No warm-cool color tension. Glow effects applied uniformly (everything glows the same). The background could be swapped for a different space image and nothing would change

### Professional Cosmic (70-80)
- 2-3 depth layers. Sacred geometry integrated into the composition (rotation, opacity control). Color palette is intentional with warm/cool relationship
- Atmospheric glow effects are specific to light sources. Star field has varying densities. The overall composition has a mood (contemplative, energetic, mystical)
- **Specific failure markers**: Still "safe" — the composition follows predictable patterns. Light sources are generic (central glow, nothing more). The color palette is competent but not memorable. Motion is uniform (everything animates at the same speed)

### Exceptional Cosmic (85-95) — THE TARGET
- Whendell-level atmospheric depth: 3+ layers with clear atmospheric perspective
- Light sources are CHARACTERS — they influence surrounding elements' colors, cast visible light through gas/dust, create the emotional center of the composition
- Sacred geometry is structural — it defines the composition's grid, not just decoration. It rotates at multiple speeds, creating mesmerizing depth
- Color palette could be named and described: "Warm Nebula with Amber Core" or "Cool Cathedral with Gold Accent." The palette IS the mood
- At least one "stop scrolling" moment — an animation timing that surprises, a gradient transition that feels three-dimensional, a parallax effect that creates genuine depth perception
- **Quality indicator**: A psytrance-savvy viewer would screenshot this and share it. A Whendell-level artist would recognize the atmospheric depth as sharing their sensibility

### Masterclass Cosmic (95-100)
- The design feels like being INSIDE a cosmic experience — not viewing a picture of space but experiencing it
- Every pixel contributes to the atmosphere. The background isn't separate from the content — they're one unified visual experience
- The sacred geometry isn't decoration — it's the architecture. Remove it and the composition loses its structure
- Motion is choreographed like music — slow sections, building intensity, moments of stillness. The scroll experience has a rhythm that mirrors a psytrance track's arc (buildup → climax → ambient → buildup)

## The "One Memorable Moment" Test

Every design above the Professional tier (70+) should have at least one element that makes someone stop scrolling:

### What Qualifies
- An unexpected animation: sacred geometry that assembles as you scroll, a nebula that responds to mouse position
- An unusually beautiful gradient: not a 2-stop linear gradient but a complex multi-layer radial gradient that creates perceived depth
- A typography moment: a heading that uses custom letter-spacing animation, or text that emerges from cosmic noise
- A composition surprise: an element that extends beyond its expected boundary, creating depth
- A loading transition: the page loads through a cosmic warp/tunnel effect, not just a spinner

### What Doesn't Qualify
- A stock animation (fade-in on scroll, parallax background) — these are EXPECTED, not memorable
- A large hero image — being big isn't memorable, being beautiful is
- Text effects for the sake of effects — glitch text, typed text, etc. that don't serve the mood
- Performance-heavy 3D scenes that lag — a memorable moment must be SMOOTH

## The CSS Ceiling

CSS gradients, SVG patterns, and animations have an absolute quality ceiling:
- Stock to Professional (40-80): achievable with CSS-only
- CEILING: ~78-80. Maximum for pure CSS, regardless of skill
- Exceptional (85-95): REQUIRES generated/curated imagery — real visual depth, not code tricks
- Masterclass (95-100): custom imagery throughout, every visual element intentional

A pure-CSS cosmic hero section will NEVER reach Exceptional tier. CSS radial-gradient is not
a nebula. box-shadow is not volumetric light. To cross the 80 ceiling, you need real imagery
— then layer CSS effects on top for polish.

## Applying Taste Calibration in Reviews

When reviewing a design, the agent should:

1. **Tier identification**: "This design is currently at the Competent tier (60)" — identify exactly where it falls
2. **Gap analysis**: "To reach Professional (75), it needs: consistent spacing system, intentional color palette with warm/cool relationship, and at least one depth layer"
3. **Next-tier specifics**: Never try to jump more than one tier at once. A Stock-level design should aim for Competent first, not Exceptional
4. **Domain calibration**: Apply the domain-specific tier definitions (cosmic tiers for psytrance projects, general tiers for other domains)
5. **The honest floor**: Be honest about the current tier. Calling Competent work "Professional" creates false calibration. The agent's value is in ACCURATE assessment, not encouragement
